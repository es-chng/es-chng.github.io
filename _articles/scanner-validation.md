issue_type: "Digital issue"
the_issue: >
  A laboratory replacing or adding a whole slide imaging (WSI) scanner or viewing platform must
  decide how much of its original diagnostic validation still applies. Published validation
  guidance addresses initial adoption of digital pathology for primary diagnosis in detail; it
  is less specific about what constitutes adequate revalidation after a subsequent platform
  change, leaving the scope of revalidation largely to local judgement.
anchors:
  - citation: "College of American Pathologists. Guideline for validating whole slide imaging for diagnostic use, 2013 (updated 2021)"
    type: "guideline"
    note: >
      Recommends a validation study of at least 60 cases per intended application, with
      pathologist comparison of digital and glass-slide diagnoses and a washout period of two
      or more weeks between modalities, for initial adoption of WSI for primary diagnosis.
  - citation: "Subsequent expert guidance addressing revalidation after a platform change (representative summary)"
    type: "expert consensus"
    note: >
      Suggests that a change limited to scanner hardware, objective, or compression algorithm
      generally warrants a smaller, focused revalidation targeted at the specific change, rather
      than repetition of the full original study, reserving a full revalidation for substantial
      platform changes; this article does not attempt to define "substantial" precisely, as the
      cited sources do not offer a single operational threshold.
  - citation: "Departmental quality assurance review of digital sign-out discordance (representative summary)"
    type: "audit"
    note: >
      In institutional quality data reviewed for this article, discordant digital diagnoses were
      more frequently attributable to image-capture artefact (out-of-focus regions, stitching
      errors, incomplete tissue capture) than to interpretation error attributable to the viewing
      software itself; this pattern should not be assumed to generalise without local audit.
solvable_now:
  - "Scope revalidation to the component that changed: a new scanner warrants image-quality and capture-fidelity checks; a viewer-only change warrants a smaller usability and display check."
  - "Select the revalidation case set to include categories the reporting pathologist has previously found diagnostically difficult on digital images (for example, faint immunohistochemistry, small foci, borderline mitotic counts), rather than a general or convenience sample."
  - "Assess whole-slide capture quality specifically -- focus across the tissue area, stitching artefact, and colour reproduction against known glass reference slides -- as a component distinct from diagnostic concordance."
  - "Document the revalidation to the same standard as the original validation, including case selection rationale, results, and the name of the pathologist providing sign-off."
not_solved:
  - "No cited source specifies a quantitative threshold distinguishing a 'minor' platform change (warranting focused revalidation) from a 'substantial' one (warranting full revalidation); this judgement is currently left to local quality assurance processes."
  - "Comparability of diagnostic confidence across different scanner platforms -- whether a diagnosis rendered confidently on one platform's output would be rendered with equal confidence on another's -- has not, to this author's knowledge, been directly quantified in the published literature."
sample_wording: >
  Platform revalidation note: imaging platform changed from [prior scanner/viewer] to [new
  scanner/viewer] on [date]. Focused revalidation comprising [n] cases across [named difficult
  categories] completed with no diagnostic discordance requiring escalation. Reviewed and
  signed off by [name], [date].
personal_perspective: >
  This author does not treat a scanner or viewer change as a routine information-technology
  event requiring no clinical input. Before signing out cases on a new platform, this author
  requests a focused case set drawn specifically from categories in which digital interpretation
  has previously been difficult in this author's own practice -- rather than accepting a vendor
  assertion of equivalence as a substitute for direct comparison against locally selected
  difficult cases.
bottom_line: >
  A change of scanner or viewing platform is not diagnostically neutral by default. Scope
  revalidation to what specifically changed, test locally difficult case categories rather than
  a general sample, and document sign-off before the new platform is used for routine reporting.
