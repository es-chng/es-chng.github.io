the_issue: >
  Mismatch repair (MMR) immunohistochemistry is used to screen colorectal and endometrial
  carcinomas for Lynch syndrome and to inform immunotherapy eligibility. Interpretation is
  generally described as binary (retained versus lost) for each of the four proteins assessed
  (MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, MSH6), but a substantial minority of cases show weak, focal, or
  heterogeneous staining that does not fit cleanly into either category, and published guidance
  on handling this pattern is less detailed than guidance on unequivocal loss.
anchors:
  - citation: "Institutional and professional-body guidance on MMR IHC interpretation (representative summary)"
    type: "guideline"
    note: >
      Recommends confirming an adequate internal positive control (non-neoplastic stroma,
      lymphocytes) before interpreting tumour staining, and repeating the stain if the control
      is weak or absent, consistent across the sources surveyed for this article.
  - citation: "Departmental audit of MMR IHC discordance following repeat staining (representative summary)"
    type: "audit"
    note: >
      In institutional audit data, a proportion of cases initially read as equivocal or
      weak/patchy resolved to a clear result on repeat staining, though the exact proportion
      varies by series and this article does not attempt to synthesise a pooled estimate.
solvable_now:
  - "Confirm the internal positive control is adequate before interpreting tumour staining; a weak or absent control invalidates interpretation of the tumour regardless of the pattern observed."
  - "If staining is weak, patchy, or heterogeneous with an adequate control, repeat the stain before finalising an interpretation, since a meaningful proportion of such cases resolve on repeat testing in the audit data available to this author."
  - "Where repeat staining does not resolve the pattern, report the finding as equivocal explicitly, rather than defaulting to either 'retained' or 'lost', and recommend molecular testing (microsatellite instability testing or MLH1 methylation analysis, as clinically appropriate) as the next step."
not_solved:
  - "No consensus threshold defines what proportion or intensity of staining still constitutes 'patchy retained' as opposed to 'patchy loss'; interpretation at the margin is described in the literature as subjective."
  - "The evidence base for how much repeat staining improves reproducibility specifically for the patchy pattern (as opposed to MMR IHC generally) is limited to institutional audit series rather than multicentre studies, in the sources reviewed for this article."
personal_perspective: >
  This author's approach to a patchy or weak result is to check the control first, repeat the
  stain if the control and pattern warrant it, and document the reasoning for the final call
  explicitly in the report, including a statement of residual uncertainty where the pattern is
  not fully resolved after repeat testing. This reflects one pathologist's working practice, set
  out here for others to weigh against their own; it is not institutional guidance and has not
  been independently reviewed.
bottom_line: >
  Patchy or weak MMR staining is not a result that should be forced into a binary retained/lost
  call. Confirm the control, repeat the stain where appropriate, and report genuine equivocation
  as such, recommending molecular follow-up rather than resolving uncertainty by assumption.
