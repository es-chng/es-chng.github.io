practical_question: >-
  In a patient with a fibroepithelial lesion of the breast, should pathologists
  use the WHO 5th edition criteria or the Refined Diagnostic Criteria (RDC) to
  classify phyllodes tumors as benign, borderline, or malignant?

why_it_matters: >-
  About 2% of phyllodes tumors metastasize and are usually fatal. The WHO 5th
  edition classifies 44% of metastatic tumors as borderline, missing their
  malignant potential. The RDC reclassifies 24% more tumors as malignant,
  eliminating borderline metastasis risk but overdiagnosing 60% of malignant
  tumors. Getting this wrong means either missed metastases or unnecessary
  aggressive surgery.

interpretation: >-
  The WHO system underdiagnoses malignant tumors: 44% of metastatic tumors are
  called borderline, and 10% of WHO-borderline tumors metastasize. The RDC
  eliminates borderline metastasis risk but classifies 24% more tumors as
  malignant, with 60% not metastasizing. This is a trade-off between sensitivity
  and specificity. The RDC is preferable for ruling out metastatic potential,
  but overdiagnosis is a real concern. The interim WHO proposal (four of five
  criteria) is a compromise but remains unvalidated.

practice_recommendations:
  - "Use the RDC when malignant phyllodes tumor is in the differential, to avoid missing metastatic potential."
  - "For borderline tumors, state which system was used and comment on the metastatic risk (10% by WHO, 0% by RDC in validation studies)."
  - "If diagnosis remains ambiguous, report as 'fibroepithelial lesion' and recommend excision."
  - "Adjust mitotic counts for microscope field size using Table 2 of the source review."

implementation_notes:
  - "Add RDC criteria to synoptic reporting templates."
  - "Document specific histologic features and the classification system used."
  - "Communicate uncertainty to the multidisciplinary team for borderline tumors."
  - "Audit classification concordance and outcomes locally."

escalation_or_exception:
  - "If spindle cell metaplastic carcinoma is in the differential, obtain additional sampling and immunohistochemistry (p63, p40, high molecular weight cytokeratins, CD34)."
  - "In re-excision specimens with reactive changes obscuring margins, sign out descriptively and recommend close follow-up."
  - "In pediatric patients, avoid overdiagnosis based on cellularity or mitotic activity alone."
  - "For well-differentiated liposarcoma as a heterologous element, note that 2 of 8 such tumors metastasized in RDC studies."

personal_perspective: >-
  I would use the RDC as the primary framework because the WHO criteria miss 44%
  of metastatic tumors. I would document both systems and communicate the
  uncertainty clearly to the multidisciplinary team. Avoiding fatal metastases
  takes priority over avoiding overtreatment, but overdiagnosis must still be
  acknowledged.

bottom_line: >-
  WHO underdiagnoses 44% of metastatic phyllodes tumors as borderline (10%
  metastasis risk). RDC reclassifies 24% more as malignant, eliminating
  borderline risk but overdiagnosing 60%. Use RDC for sensitivity, document both,
  and communicate uncertainty.
