issue_type: "Reporting issue"
the_issue: >
  A colonic polyp shows cytological and architectural atypia exceeding conventional high-grade
  dysplasia, with foci suggestive of invasion limited to the lamina propria. Colorectal reporting
  guidelines and general oncological usage diverge on nomenclature for this appearance: whether
  it should be termed intramucosal adenocarcinoma (as in some Western and most East Asian
  practice) or classified with high-grade dysplasia (as recommended by the WHO and the
  International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting, ICCR). The label chosen has been shown to
  influence subsequent clinical management independent of the underlying histology.
anchors:
  - citation: "International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting. Colorectal Cancer Histopathology Reporting Guide, 1st edn, 2020"
    type: "guideline"
    note: >
      States that neoplasia confined to the lamina propria (intramucosal carcinoma) should be
      classified with high-grade dysplasia, citing negligible metastatic potential due to the
      near-absence of lymphatics within the colonic mucosa. pTis is explicitly not recognised
      in this system.
  - citation: "Retrospective multicentre cohort, colorectal IMC vs HGD vs T1 CRC, Gastrointest Endosc 2024"
    type: "cohort study"
    note: >
      In a cohort in which no lymph node metastases were identified among patients with
      intramucosal carcinoma (0 of 363 nodes sampled), the adjusted odds of surgical management
      were nonetheless significantly higher for patients labelled with intramucosal carcinoma
      than for those labelled high-grade dysplasia (adjusted OR 2.62, 95% CI 1.18-5.85), despite
      similar rates of synchronous and metachronous advanced neoplasia between groups.
  - citation: "Vink-Börger E, et al. Is it time to acknowledge intramucosal colorectal carcinoma? Histopathology 2025"
    type: "cohort study"
    note: >
      Applying strict histological criteria to an archival cohort (1990-2024), the authors
      identified 14 cases meeting a restrictive definition of true intramucosal carcinoma
      (estimated incidence 0.01% in the screened population), of which half were associated
      with microsatellite instability; outcomes in this subset were favourable. The authors
      argue for retaining the diagnostic category under strict criteria, a position that
      diverges from the ICCR and WHO recommendation above.
solvable_now:
  - "The management-relevant question is whether invasion has crossed the muscularis mucosae into the submucosa, not which label is applied to mucosa-confined atypia; this distinction should be stated explicitly in the report regardless of terminology used."
  - "Reporting terminology should follow the standard in use at the reporting institution or jurisdiction (for example, ICCR/WHO usage favours high-grade dysplasia; other systems, including common Japanese practice, use intramucosal carcinoma for histologically equivalent lesions)."
  - "Because a biopsy cannot exclude deeper invasion beyond the sampled tissue, complete endoscopic excision with full histological examination of the specimen is the relevant recommendation independent of which label is used."
  - "Given the documented association between the carcinoma label and increased odds of surgical referral even without a difference in oncological outcome, an explicit statement of the management implication in the report may reduce the risk of the label alone driving overtreatment."
not_solved:
  - "Outcomes for poorly differentiated or signet-ring-cell intramucosal lesions are described in case-series-level evidence only; no cohort-level data quantify their risk relative to conventional intramucosal adenocarcinoma."
  - "Whether the minority of cases meeting strict criteria for true intramucosal carcinoma (as opposed to high-grade dysplasia) constitute a biologically distinct entity warranting separate reporting remains contested between the cited guideline and cohort sources."
  - "No prospective study has directly tested whether an explicit statement of management implication in the report measurably reduces the surgical-referral discrepancy documented in retrospective cohorts."
personal_perspective: >
  This author's practice follows ICCR/WHO terminology (high-grade dysplasia) for lesions confined
  to the lamina propria, reserving "adenocarcinoma" for cases with unequivocal submucosal invasion.
  Where morphology is unusual enough to raise the question of true intramucosal carcinoma under
  strict criteria, that observation is documented descriptively in the report body rather than
  resolved through a change in the diagnostic line, and is discussed directly with the referring
  endoscopist given the evidence that terminology alone can influence the management pathway
  chosen.
bottom_line: >
  Neoplasia confined to the colonic mucosa carries a low reported risk of nodal involvement across
  the cited cohorts, but the terminology applied to it has been independently associated with
  higher odds of surgical management. Reports should state invasion depth explicitly and specify
  the management implication, using the terminology convention of the reporting institution.
